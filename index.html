      Lessons from Grandma

      Old-fashioned habits worth keeping

      • Read the recipe twice before you start, and get everything out.
      • Cook with the seasons and use what’s good and affordable.
      • Waste nothing: bones make stock, stale bread makes crumbs.
      • Taste as you go and season a little at a time.
      • Set the table properly, even for a simple supper.
      Make it a tradition

      Start your own family recipe box

      • Ask the family cooks to write down the dishes everyone loves.
      • Add the stories: who made it, and when it was served.
      • Note your changes, like “less sugar” or “double the gravy”.
      • Cook together and pass the skills on to the next generation.

      Kitchen questions

      Homestyle cooking FAQ

      What makes a meal “homestyle”?

      Homestyle cooking is the food many of us grew up with: roasts, stews, soups, bakes and pies made with everyday ingredients, cooked without fuss and meant for sharing around a family table.

      Can I make these recipes ahead of time?

      Many of them get better with time. Stews, soups and pasta bakes can be made a day ahead and reheated until piping hot. Pies and bread are best on the day but freeze well.

      How do I know when roast meat is cooked?

      A meat thermometer is the most reliable way. Chicken and turkey should reach 74°C (165°F) in the thickest part; whole cuts of beef, pork and lamb are safe at 63°C (145°F) followed by a rest.

      What if I don’t have the right size baking pan?

      Use our pan size converter. It works out how much to scale the recipe and how to adjust baking time when you swap, say, a round cake tin for a square or rectangular one.

      Can I swap ingredients in old family recipes?

      Often, yes. Our ingredient swap finder covers common substitutes such as buttermilk, self-raising flour, eggs in baking and fresh versus dried herbs.
