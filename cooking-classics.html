Cooking classics every home cook should know

The timeless skills behind homestyle meals: roasting, making proper gravy, slow-cooking stews, pastry, bread and a few tricks from the family kitchen.

01Roasting

The art of the roast

A good roast starts with meat at room temperature, a hot oven to begin with and plenty of seasoning. Most importantly, it ends with a rest: covering the meat loosely with foil for 15–30 minutes lets the juices settle, making it easier to carve and far juicier.

02Gravy

Proper pan gravy

  1. Lift the roast out and spoon off most of the fat, leaving the dark juices.
  2. Set the tin over a low heat and stir in 2 tablespoons of plain flour, scraping up the sticky bits.
  3. Gradually whisk in 500 ml hot stock and simmer for 5 minutes until smooth and glossy.
  4. Season, add any resting juices and strain into a warm jug.
03Stews & soups

Slow cooking secrets

  • Brown in batches so the meat sears rather than steams.
  • Build a base of onion, carrot and celery cooked slowly until sweet.
  • Keep it at a gentle simmer, never a rolling boil, for tender meat.
  • Thicken at the end if needed with a little cornflour mixed with cold water.
  • Make it ahead: flavours deepen overnight in the fridge.
04Pastry

Shortcrust pastry by hand

Rub 125 g cold diced butter into 250 g plain flour with a pinch of salt until it looks like breadcrumbs. Add 3–4 tablespoons of ice-cold water and bring together without kneading. Wrap and chill for 30 minutes before rolling.

  • Keep everything cold, including your hands.
  • Handle the dough as little as possible for a tender crust.
  • Blind-bake tart cases to avoid a soggy bottom.
05Bread

Signs your bread is ready

  • Kneaded enough: the dough is smooth and springs back when poked.
  • Risen enough: it has doubled and a floured finger leaves a dent that slowly fills.
  • Baked through: the loaf is deep golden and sounds hollow when tapped underneath.
